## Problem Statement

Prove, in under 24 hours and on one laptop, that the vibepult loop works end to end: drag perceptual faders on a mixing console, get the nearest metal band, hear it through a Spotify embed, watch the desk re-skin. The focus is fast and smooth iteration, not completeness. The one dimension that gets compromised is band count.

## What Makes This Cool

- The desk is both the question and the answer. The fader is the request, the LED meter beside it is where the band really sits. The gap is the most useful thing on screen.
- The space is measured, not tagged. Elderwind and Mayhem sit at opposite ends of a serenity axis that nothing in their genre tag predicts.
- Later (not in the 24h, from the second opinion): run the extractor on any dropped clip and watch the faders animate to where that clip sits. The console becomes a mirror as well as a search box, and every clip you disagree with is a labelled test case.

## Constraints

Inherited from the parent doc and `CLAUDE.md`, still binding in the PoC:

- No Spotify user login, no Spotify audio or analysis. Never store lyric text. Raw per-band features immutable, versioned by extractor. Weight matrix in a config file. iTunes by album, about 20 requests per minute.

PoC-specific, decided this session:

- **No Spotify developer app at all.** Playback is the Spotify artist embed (`open.spotify.com/embed/artist/{id}`). Expected behaviour, **unverified until step 0**: it lists the artist's popular tracks itself and plays with the listener's own session inside the iframe. We need one artist ID per band, pasted by hand. The parent doc's Cloudflare Worker and top-tracks proxy are not built; see Open Questions for the decision rule on whether they are ever needed.
- **Laptop only.** `python -m http.server` from `web/`. No hosting, no PWA, no CI.
- **Toolchain as found on this Mac:** Python 3.11 (conda), node 22, no ffmpeg, no uv. iTunes previews are AAC `.m4a`; macOS ships `/usr/bin/afconvert`, which decodes them to WAV with zero installs. Verified live this session, as was iTunes artist → albums → songs with preview URLs and no key. Deezer previews are MP3 and also need no key.
- **Fast iteration is a hard requirement.** Editing `weights.yaml` and seeing the desk change must take seconds and touch no audio.

## Premises

Agreed, with premise 1 revised after the second opinion:

1. **The riskiest assumption is measurability, tested two ways with numeric gates written before any number exists** (the gates are below under Success Criteria). (a) Pole pairs: Elderwind above Mayhem on serenity, Anaal Nathrakh above Metallica on aggression, each by a stated percentile gap. (b) Collapse and confounds: four loud, distorted, similarly mastered bands (Mayhem, Gorgoroth, Cannibal Corpse, Anaal Nathrakh) must not collapse onto adjacent ranks on at least two of the six audio faders (aggression, serenity, darkness, rawness, speed, heaviness); that gate catches collapse only. Separately, pool-wide confound gates require blended aggression to have absolute Spearman rho at most 0.8 against loudness alone and against original release year; those catch a space that is really measuring mastering era. All gates run on audio features only, with metadata inputs such as the vocals field zeroed out of the blends, so a pass cannot come from a genre prior. They live in `check.py`, which runs after every publish.
2. **The UI ships whole.** All four control types, bypass per control, LED meters, reference-band ticks, result panel with embed, theme switch, desktop and mobile. The user's words: the UI stays, the whole thing end to end.
3. **Processing ships whole, pool stays small.** The ingest package implements the parent doc's pipeline as far as each source allows (the feature table below says exactly how far), over 16 bands at the poles rather than 100.
4. **No Worker, no Spotify app.** See Constraints.
5. **Per-clip raw features are cached on disk once and committed.** Publish reads cached JSON, never audio. That is the parent doc's immutability rule applied per clip.

Ruled out during the session: a desk over hand-scored fader values (proves nothing about measurability); a six-fader minimal page (user wants the whole loop).

## Cross-Model Perspective

Independent cold read by a Claude subagent (Sonnet), no conversation context. Kept:

- **afconvert** to WAV in the fetch step; the decode problem is the first thing that bites. Independently verified here.
- **Pass/fail before the desk**, because the alternative is tuning weights until it feels right, which overfits to vibes.
- **Premise 1 was too weak.** The pole-pair test is one-dimensional (acoustic versus blast beats). The real risk is discriminating inside the loud-distorted cluster where most of metal lives, and the loudness war flattens production features into a mastering-era signal. Cheap confound tests: rank-correlate blended aggression against loudness alone and against release year. Adopted.
- **Scatter view of the pool** (2D projection, the nearest dot moves as you drag) exposes failures of the space that a single result band hides. About 40 lines of SVG. Stretch goal.
- **Two-way instrument** (drop a clip, faders animate). Deferred past the PoC.

Not kept for the 24h: Essentia-tensorflow or CLAP pretrained mood heads as a competing baseline. arm64 wheels are flaky and CLAP drags in torch; either can burn three hours on install. Logged as an M2 comparator: if a hand-tuned librosa blend cannot beat `mood_aggressive` on our own pole pairs, that decides whether the weight-matrix approach deserves the rest of the parent doc.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Flat scripts + static page
Four scripts, six faders, 12 bands. Effort S. Rejected: user wants the whole loop end to end.

### Approach B: Real M1 + M2 layout, complete UI, small pool (chosen)
Installable `ingest` package with iTunes and Deezer, extractor versions and a per-band record schema; publish step; complete console in `web/`. Effort M (human: about a week / CC: 14h plus 3h contingency). Risk Med: more plumbing before the desk appears.

### Approach C: A plus a pretrained mood baseline
Essentia or CLAP comparator column in the check script. Deferred to M2 for install risk.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B, as chosen.

### Pipeline per band

Resolve from `pool.yaml` → iTunes albums, then one song lookup per band with all album ids comma-joined (approved D6) → pick about 12 clips spread across full-length albums, dedupe live and remaster by title → any track without an iTunes preview is looked up on Deezer by artist, title and duration (within 3 s) → afconvert to WAV → audio features per clip → Metal Archives page for metadata → Genius lyrics per track for lyric features, text discarded → aggregate to one record. Each clip record carries its source (`itunes` or `deezer`) and release year. Per-clip JSON is keyed by extractor version, so re-running is a no-op unless the extractor changes.

**Coverage** is confirmed in step 0 for all 16 bands and means: at least 8 previewable tracks from full-length albums across iTunes and Deezer, a Metal Archives band page, and an artist embed that loads. A band failing any of these is replaced before ground truth is written. After sealing there is no swapping; a band that still ends up under 8 clips in step 2 is kept, marked `partial: true` in its record, and listed by `check.py`. If a protected band goes partial, the gate that names it is reported as not evaluable rather than passed, and the user decides whether to substitute.

**Codec mix.** iTunes clips are AAC, Deezer clips MP3, and spectral features are codec-sensitive. `check.py` prints the Deezer share per band and flags any band above 50 percent.

**Release year** per band is the median original release year of the albums the clips come from, taken from the Metal Archives discography page (one extra fetch per band, inside the scraper timebox) by album title, with iTunes `releaseDate` as fallback. Title matching (approved D4): normalise both sides by lowercasing, stripping diacritics, dropping bracketed text and a stop list of reissue words (remastered, remaster, deluxe, edition, anniversary, expanded, bonus, version, reissue), and collapsing punctuation and whitespace; then exact match, else `difflib` ratio ≥ 0.9. An unmatched album takes the iTunes year and its clips carry `year_source: itunes`; `check.py` prints the count. `check.py` also flags any clip whose iTunes year exceeds the matched Metal Archives year by more than two, since iTunes dates on catalogue bands are often reissue dates.

### Fader status (expected; `publish` prints the computed status per fader and `check.py` fails if it differs from this table)

| Fader | Without Genius token | With token |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aggression, Serenity, Darkness, Humanity | live, audio and metadata inputs only, weights renormalised | live, partial: the parent definitions' lyric-theme inputs are replaced by vocabulary richness and density where they make sense; themes and sentiment are M2 |
| Grandeur, Rawness, Speed, Heaviness, Melody, Technicality, Consistency | live | live |
| Theatricality | live from 26, 27 and metadata; 28 dropped | same |
| Earth vs cosmos, Despair vs hope | deferred to M2 with features 20 and 21; not rendered in the PoC | same |

Rule for a fader with missing inputs, applied pool-wide so distances stay comparable: a lyric feature enters a blend only if all 16 bands have it; otherwise it is dropped for every band and `check.py` lists the bands that caused the drop. Then, if at least half of the fader's absolute weight mass is present, the remaining weights are renormalised; otherwise the fader starts bypassed. The lyric features that remain in the PoC (vocabulary richness, language, density) work in any language. The expected statuses in this table are also committed as `expected_status` keys in `weights.yaml`; a status flip during tuning fails `check.py` until the expectation is updated deliberately. `check.py` gates (pole pairs, cluster spread, confounds) run against audio features only, so they give the same answer with or without the token and cannot be passed by metadata priors. The vocals field (feature 17) comes from Metal Archives lineup roles with the subgenre as fallback; it is a prior, labelled as such, used by the desk and excluded from the gates. The ground-truth report runs against whatever definition is live.

### Pool

16 bands, chosen so every gate has something to bite on and at least six theme packs appear: Elderwind, Agalloch, Alcest (serene pole); Mayhem, Gorgoroth, Cannibal Corpse, Anaal Nathrakh (the brutal cluster); Metallica, Iron Maiden, Black Sabbath (mid anchors); Nightwish, Rhapsody of Fire (grandeur); Ghost (theatrical); Candlemass (doom); Rammstein (industrial); Meshuggah (technicality, djent). Protected from replacement because the gates name them: Elderwind, Mayhem, Anaal Nathrakh, Metallica, Gorgoroth, Cannibal Corpse. If a protected band fails coverage in step 0, the gate is rewritten with a substitute of the same pole before ground truth is sealed.

**Theme packs.** `themes.yaml` maps the Metal Archives genre string by first keyword match, in this order: black → Black; death → Death; doom → Doom; folk, pagan, viking or medieval → Folk; industrial → Industrial; power or symphonic → Power; thrash → Thrash; prog, djent or technical → Prog; anything else → Default steel. Expected result over the pool: Black (Elderwind, Agalloch, Alcest, Mayhem, Gorgoroth, Anaal Nathrakh), Death (Cannibal Corpse), Doom (Candlemass, Black Sabbath), Industrial (Rammstein), Power (Nightwish, Rhapsody of Fire), Thrash (Metallica), Prog (Meshuggah), Default (Iron Maiden, Ghost). That is seven packs; `check.py` counts distinct packs and fails below six. Each pack is a CSS-variable set: panel, panel inset, fader cap, LED on and off, label, accent, a display font, and two shape slots, `--cap-shape` (rect, pill, chamfer) and `--led-shape` (round, square, bar). Pack rule (design review D12): every non-default pack sets a display font different from steel and at least one shape slot different from steel, plus its colours, so a committed change is a change of type and shape, not only hue. `check.py` fails any pack whose label on panel contrast is under 4.5:1 or whose LED on versus LED off contrast is under 3:1 (WCAG relative luminance over the hex values in `themes.yaml`). Steel's values are in the Console UI section.

### Per-band record schema

Validated by `check.py` on every publish.

```json
{
  "slug": "elderwind",
  "name": "Elderwind",
  "extractor_version": "0.1.0",
  "ids": {"itunes": 0, "deezer": 0, "spotify": "..."},
  "metadata": {"formed": 2011, "split": null, "country": "RU", "status": "active", "members": 1,
               "past_members": 0, "full_lengths": 4, "genre": "Atmospheric Black Metal",
               "vocals": "harsh", "instrumental": false, "fans": 0, "release_year": 2020},
  "partial": false,
  "clips": ["elderwind/<albumId>-<n>", "..."],
  "features": {"tempo_bpm": {"median": 0.0, "iqr": 0.0}, "lufs": {"median": 0.0, "iqr": 0.0}},
  "lyrics": {"ttr": {"median": 0.0, "iqr": 0.0}, "language": "ru", "wpm": {"median": 0.0, "iqr": 0.0}},
  "hand": {"persona": 0.2, "camp": 0.1},
  "sources_failed": []
}
```

`features` has one entry per computed audio feature; the required keys are the computed and proxy rows of the feature table (tempo_bpm, onset_rate, tempo_var, low_onset_rate, lufs, rms_dbfs, crest, centroid, flatness, sub100_share, lowest_pitch, harmonic_share, clean_share, minor_share, chord_rate, repetitiveness, vocal_share, vocal_voiced, song_length). `lyrics` is optional and absent when there is no token or Genius failed for every track; `language` is the modal per-track language. `hand` is required. The clip record (`schema/clip.json`) requires clip id, band slug, source, album, original year, extractor_version and the same feature keys as single values. Lyric fetches cover the 12 clip tracks only. Their per-track derived features are cached, so an aggregation change never refetches; because the text is discarded, any change to how features are derived bumps lyrics_version and does refetch. `sources_failed` lists any source that errored for this band; `check.py` prints it so partial records are never silent.

### Console UI

SVG and CSS, vanilla ES modules, no bundler: the iteration loop is a reload and the parent doc's runtime is static anyway. Faders and knobs with pointer events, switches as LEDs, selectors as native `<select>`, bypass button per control, LED meter per fader lit to the result band's percentile, reference-band tick labels, percentile on hover, result panel with band name and artist embed, a next button for second and third nearest, theme pack as a CSS-variable set per subgenre with an opacity cross-fade, mobile layout with pinned result over a horizontally scrolling desk. Minimum mobile deliverable that is never cut: pinned result panel and touch drag on faders with `touch-action: none`; knob gestures, horizontal desk scroll polish and the compact embed height are the polish that can be cut. Theme packs are palettes only; generated textures are M4.

#### Layout and hierarchy (design review, 2026-10-05, D3 and D4)

What the eye hits, in order: the band name, then the faders and their meters, then the embed, then the filter block (knobs, switches, selectors). The result panel is the desk's master section on the right, as on a real console, so desk and player share one screen on a 1440 × 900 laptop.

#### Interaction states (design review, 2026-10-05, D5 to D7)

**Update cadence (D5).** Live during a drag, every frame: LED meters, band name, subgenre line. Committed on pointer up or keyboard key release: the embed swap and the theme cross-fade, and only when the nearest slug differs from the last committed slug. A drag that returns to the same band commits nothing. Playback is never interrupted mid-drag. `engine.test.js` asserts one commit per gesture and no commit when the slug is unchanged.

**First load (D6).** All twelve faders at 50, every knob, switch and selector bypassed, the nearest band computed and shown with its embed as soon as `bands.json` loads. One hint line under the band name, in the label colour, until the first pointer or key interaction: *Drag a fader. The desk answers.* The first-load band is deterministic and is the manual smoke test.

**Embed slot (D7).** Fixed height (352 px desktop, see mobile below) so the panel never jumps. While the iframe loads, a themed placeholder shows the band name and *Loading Spotify*. If the iframe has not fired `load` within 5 s the placeholder becomes an *Open in Spotify* link to `open.spotify.com/artist/{id}`. The same link is always present under the embed as a small secondary action, so a blocked iframe (ad blocker, http LAN origin, Spotify down) is never a dead end.

#### Journey and motion (design review, 2026-10-05, D8)

One authored motion moment, on the commit, and nothing else animates. On a committed band change: the theme cross-fade runs 400 ms ease-out (opacity only, as the parent doc requires); the band name swaps with a 200 ms fade; the meters settle to the new band's percentiles with a 150 ms ease. Live meter updates during a drag are immediate, no easing. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` all three are instant.

| Step | User does | User feels | Plan specifies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (5 s) | Opens the page | curiosity: a desk, a band already playing, one hint line | D6 first load, D3 hierarchy, D12 load failure |
| 2 | Drags Aggression up | agency: meters move under the hand, name flips live | D5 live cadence, reference ticks teach the scale |
| 3 | Releases | the reward: skin fades, name reveals, meters land | D8 motion, D5 commit rule |
| 4 | Reads the gap between fader and meter | insight: where the match compromised | parent doc display section, meters lit to percentile |
| 5 (5 min) | Flips filters, hits Next, finds the empty state | control, then a nudge back | empty filter copy, greyed options, Next labelled with the band |
| 6 | Embed never loads on the phone | mild annoyance, then an exit | D7 fallback link |
| 7 (reflective) | Edits weights.yaml, reloads | ownership: the space is theirs to tune | iteration loop under 5 s, check.py gates |

The arc breaks nowhere by specification now; it can still break in execution if seven palette-only packs read as a colour picker (see Pass 5, pack rule).

#### Character rules (design review, 2026-10-05, D9)

Surface class: OPERATE (an instrument you finish a task on) with an EXPERIENCE first viewport (the desk is the work). No cards, no hero, no section rhythm; the console is one composition. Selectors (D9) stay native `<select>` elements and their closed state is themed from the pack tokens (`appearance: none`, panel background, label colour, engraved inset, pack-coloured chevron); the open list stays native so keyboard, screen reader and the mobile picker come free. Browser surfaces are themed from the pack: focus ring (2 px accent, offset 2 px), selection colour, scrollbar on the mobile desk. No glow, no zero-offset halos, no decorative shadows: depth comes from the panel inset and the cap's one offset shadow.

## Success Criteria

Gates in `check.py`, in percentile points (0 to 100) unless stated. Written here before any extraction and not changed once numbers exist.

`check.py` has two result classes (approved D5). **FAIL** exits 1: a record fails the schema; an evaluable gate below fails; a fader's computed status differs from `expected_status` in `weights.yaml`; fewer than six distinct theme packs; a pack failing the contrast rule (label on panel under 4.5:1, LED on versus off under 3:1) or a non-default pack not changing font and a shape slot from steel (design review D12); a `short` label over 9 characters (design review D11); ground truth unsealed while `data/clips/` has commits. **WARN** exits 0 and prints under a short `FLAGS` block at the end of the output: Deezer clip share over 50 percent for a band; a clip whose iTunes year exceeds the matched Metal Archives year by more than two; a partial band; a protected band partial, which also marks the gate naming it as not evaluable. The iteration loop `publish && check` is expected to pass with warnings present; the PoC success criteria additionally require every gate evaluable. A clip that fails to download or decode is dropped and counts toward the eight-clip minimum like any missing clip. Sealing means the earliest commit touching `data/ground-truth.yaml` is an ancestor of, or the same as, the earliest commit touching `data/clips/`; before any clips commit exists, the ground-truth row reports pending rather than failing.

| --- | --- |
| Pole pair, serenity | Elderwind minus Mayhem ≥ 40 |
| Pole pair, aggression | Anaal Nathrakh minus Metallica ≥ 30 |
| Brutal cluster spread | on at least two of the six audio faders (aggression, serenity, darkness, rawness, speed, heaviness), the range across the four bands ≥ 40 percentile points. Beside each confound gate `check.py` prints an informational baseline (approved D8): the Spearman rho of the sealed ground-truth aggression against LUFS and against original release year over the eight scored bands, so a failed gate can be read as the blend tracking loudness or year, or as the pool itself being shaped that way. The 0.8 ceiling stays fixed.

Statistical honesty: 16 bands and 8 ground-truth bands give rank correlations with wide intervals. These gates are tripwires that catch a broken space, not proof that the space is right. Weights are tuned against the same 16 bands the gates run on, in sample by design; out-of-sample validation is M2's job when the pool grows and the gates can be re-run on held-out bands.

## Implementation learnings (2026-10-05)

What building the PoC and growing it to 666 bands taught, for whoever picks this up next. Numbers are from this repository's runs.

### Data sources

- **Metal Archives** sits behind a Cloudflare challenge that rejects Python's TLS fingerprint (HTTP 403) and lets `curl` through, so `sources/http.py` shells out to `/usr/bin/curl` for every source. Band pages also answer at `bands/_/<numeric id>`, which is all Wikidata stores. About 1,300 page fetches at 3 s spacing never got blocked.
- **Metal Archives fields need care.** "Formed in" can be `N/A` (we use the first release instead). "Years active" for an on-hold or unknown band often ends in a bare start year, which made Turisas look like a one-year band with four albums; only a split-up band gets a split year, never earlier than its last studio album. Titles can be bilingual (`Russian / English`) and must match on either side.
- **iTunes albums that match no Metal Archives full-length are not full-lengths.** Every unmatched album we inspected was an EP, single, compilation, piano or remix set, or an anniversary edition, so they are skipped rather than given the iTunes year (D4 amended). Normalise away reissue subtitles after a colon or dash. iTunes years are often reissue dates (119 bands flagged); the original year comes from Metal Archives.
- **iTunes records are not uniform.** Some tracks lack `releaseDate`, some names use characters that do not normalise (`œ`), and Wikidata's Apple Music IDs are sometimes stale (Myrkur), so admission always searches by name as well and picks the artist whose albums overlap the Metal Archives discography.
- **Wikidata is the candidate source at scale.** One SPARQL query (Metal Archives P1952 plus Spotify P1902, with Apple Music P2850, Deezer P2722, genres, country and sitelinks) returns about 2,150 bands; exclude humans (solo artists have no country of origin) and fall back to the formation place's country. Its genre labels are good enough to sort bands into corners.
- **Spotify without an app works.** IDs came from the Spotify connector and were verified through the public oEmbed endpoint. The artist embed lists top tracks and plays previews when logged out. It has no volume control (the iFrame API exposes none), and its compact height is 80 px; 56 px cut off the play button.

### Pipeline engineering

- **Long runs must survive one bad record.** Admission crashed at band 87 on one odd iTunes track; now any unexpected error rejects that candidate with the error as the reason. Every decision is logged (`admission.yaml`), so a stopped run resumes where it left off. Admission ran at about 13 s per band: 643 admitted, 77 rejected (mostly no matching iTunes catalogue or under 8 previews).
- **Audio is the disk budget.** A decoded WAV is about 1.3 MB per clip (about 18 GB for 666 bands). Keep only the compressed previews (7.9 GB) and decode to a temporary WAV per extraction; an extractor change then needs no re-download.
- **macOS multiprocessing spawns fresh workers** that import the worker function by module name, which a function in `ingest/__main__.py` cannot offer; hence `ingest/clips_.py`. Extraction runs at about 2 s per clip, roughly 30 minutes for 7,600 clips on 10 workers.
- **Piping a long job into `head` kills it** (SIGPIPE after the first line). One band-record rebuild silently did nothing that way.
- **Compile JSON Schema validators once.** Validating 8,000 clip records with `jsonschema.validate` took 47 s; a compiled validator brings publish plus check back to 3.7 s for 666 bands.
- **`python -m http.server` lets the browser cache JS and CSS**, so after an edit a plain reload can run stale code, which looks like a bug that is not there. `web/serve.py` serves with caching off. Also, navigating to a URL that differs only in its hash does not reload the page.
- **Numeric names are quoted in YAML** (`slug: '1349'`); line-oriented edits of `pool.yaml` must allow for that.
- **An iTunes lookup stops at 200 rows without saying so** (review, 2026-10-05). One song lookup per band (D6) silently drops the later albums of a band with more than about 16 full-lengths, so `songs()` now asks for 12 albums per request. Transient source errors (curl failure, 403, 429, 5xx) stop `admit` instead of being logged as permanent rejections; a 404 is still a rejection.
- **A switch every band answers the same way is left off the desk.** Instrumental matched 0 of 666 bands (it is derived from the genre string), so `publish` drops any switch with one value across the pool rather than shipping a control that can only answer "No band matches". The share link's control count changes with it, so links from before the change fall back to the first-load desk.
- **pool.yaml is the source of truth for name, Spotify id and hand scores at publish time.** Records keep what was measured; `publish && check` honours a hand-score edit without re-extracting.

### Measurement

- **Judge features against the audio before any weights.** Three 0.1.0 features were broken in ways only the real pool showed. `clean_share` was about 0 for every band, because 30-second previews come from the loud middle of songs and never had a quiet passage. `lowest_pitch` returned its nothing-found value (200 Hz) for the most downtuned bands, because a 12 dB peak test rejects every low peak in a dense mix. `chord_rate` measures distortion, not harmony: rho +0.53 against spectral flatness, and brutal death and slam top the chart.
- **Melody and voice cannot be measured from a full mix with what we have.** Chroma-based pitch motion scores Napalm Death like Helloween; pYIN on the harmonic component calls Sunn O))) as voiced as Stratovarius. The Melodic and Human faders therefore rest on tone colour plus a genre-name guess for vocals, which yields absurdities (Skyforger near 0 on Melodic, Sunn O))) the most human band). The plan's note that vocal pitch range needs stems was right: a vocals stem (Demucs, parent doc M3) is the likely fix. **Decided 2026-10-07: stems, see Humanity below; Melodic not yet moved onto them.**
- **The genre-based vocals prior is wrong wherever the keywords mislead**: "black" in a folk band's genre makes it harsh, and drone has no keyword, so it reads as clean and not instrumental.
- **The serenity premise fails on audio.** Elderwind minus Mayhem was 12.5, then −4.5 and −4.2 (needs 40) across three runs. With working features, Elderwind's tremolo wall measures as noisy as Mayhem's. Either the features miss what makes it calm, or gut and audio disagree about that band. Darkness also runs against the ground truth (rho −0.71). Aggression, serenity (as a fader), rawness, speed and heaviness agree (+0.45 to +0.81).
- **The serenity failure was the weight vector, not the features (2026-10-06).** Per-input percentiles over the 666 pool showed the three heaviest serenity inputs all pulling Elderwind below Mayhem: `clean_share` (weight 2.0; tonal frames under flatness 0.005 almost never occur in a reverb-washed tremolo wall, Elderwind sits at the 10th percentile; rho against the sheet only 0.54), `lufs` (−1.0; Mayhem's masters are 3 dB quieter than Elderwind's 2019 master, so loudness is mastering era and its rho against gut serenity is −0.45) and `tempo_bpm` (−0.5; octave errors on tremolo picking, Elderwind's tempo IQR is the pool's highest; rho 0.16). Together they contributed −61 percentile points against +59 from the rest, hence the −4 gap. The inputs that track the sheet were unused by serenity: `repetitiveness` (−0.90), `low_onset_rate` (−0.82), `centroid` (−0.79), `chord_rate` (−0.74). Serenity is now harmonic share minus low-end hits, chord changes, centroid, flatness and chroma self-similarity. Gap 49.8, stable within 45 to 53 when any one weight moves by 0.5; sheet rho 0.81 → 0.94 and on the eight blind rows 0.72 → 0.93, so this is not a fit to the eight rows written after the first run. Residual: mid anchors read too serene (Metallica 73 against gut 35, Iron Maiden 67 against 20), as before. Lesson: a weight vector written from input names (clean, quiet, slow) needs the per-input table against the sheet before the first gate run; the sheet existed for exactly this and nobody read it input by input.
- **Humanity had no input that hears a voice (2026-10-06).** The desk's most human band was Sunn O))).
